# Hard-to-Vary Explanations

## Standard

A good explanation is not merely compatible with observations. It specifies a causal mechanism whose central parts cannot be freely changed while preserving the same explanatory work. Hard-to-vary is comparative evidence of explanatory quality, not proof of truth or uniqueness.

## Required Causal Account

A candidate must state the target phenomenon and observations; a short causal chain from physical mechanism to target; why every core component is necessary; what follows if each part is removed, substituted, or parameter-shifted; which serious rival fails where; and a risky observation or intervention that could count against it.

## Stress Tests

1. **Parameter substitution:** alter a central threshold, timescale, coupling, or state variable; require a specific changed outcome.
2. **Component ablation:** remove a proposed part; state what explanatory work and observable consequence are lost.
3. **Rival equivalence:** replace the causal core with a simpler rival; identify the observation or intervention now unexplained.
4. **Cross-state comparison:** require one account across relevant states, failures, recovery, or contexts.
5. **Causal-core compression:** reduce the theory to its minimal causal chain; concepts removable without prediction loss are decorative.

## Failure Modes

Reject or demote: relabeling a target as access, report, attention, or integration; claiming an unexplained identity; generic appeals to complexity or information flow; flexible metaphors; and post-hoc accommodation of adverse results.

## Selection Rule

Rank candidates by necessary mechanism, distinctive counterfactuals, discriminating risk, and low accommodation cost. Preserve imaginative candidates in an archive, but promote only candidates that survive these tests.
