# Explanatory Selection

## Ideal State

Advance only candidates that give a comparatively hard-to-vary causal explanation of the target phenomenon. A survivor is explicit about what it explains, what physical mechanism does the work, why its parts matter, how it can fail, and what remains unexplained.

## Survivor Artifact

For every candidate, provide:

1. **Target:** phenomenon and observations, defined independently of the candidate vocabulary.
2. **Causal core:** a short causal chain from physical mechanism to target outcome.
3. **Necessary roles:** every core component, relation, or parameter and its explanatory job.
4. **Counterfactuals:** component-ablation and parameter-substitution effects.
5. **Rival comparison:** a simpler or serious rival and a target observation it does not explain.
6. **Risky prediction:** a discriminating outcome that could demote or falsify the candidate.
7. **Cross-context behavior:** predictions across relevant states, perturbations, development, failure, or recovery.
8. **Remaining residue:** what is still unexplained.

## Gate

Run parameter substitution, component ablation, rival equivalence, cross-state comparison, and causal-core compression. Reject candidates that only restate labels, explain only report/access/attention/integration when the target differs, invoke an unfalsifiable intrinsic property, declare an identity without causal consequences, or retain fit by arbitrary post-hoc variation.

## Output

Return a ranked survivor portfolio with causal core, hard-to-vary score, stress-test outcomes, rival comparison, risky prediction, cross-context predictions, and remaining residue. Rank explanatory quality separately from empirical support and rhetorical appeal.
